"I'll start properly from Monday." Why Monday never comes, and the 10-minute rule that gets you started tonight

In one line Waiting for the perfect start is the most common way to lose a week. A start so small it feels silly, done every day, beats a big plan that begins next Monday.

You know the feeling. The exam date is getting closer, the syllabus looks huge, and today already feels half wasted. So you make a deal with yourself: new timetable, fresh notebook, a proper start from Monday.

Then Monday comes with a family function, a mock test that went badly, or just a tired head. The plan was so big that missing one part feels like the whole thing failed. So it moves to the next Monday.

The fix is to make the first step almost too small to skip. In Padhai Timer, a day joins your streak once you study at least 10 minutes. Ten minutes is one topic, a few questions, one page of notes. Anyone can do that tonight, even on a bad day.

Once the timer is running, ten minutes can quietly turn into forty. The next day, you don't want to break the streak or watch the D-day number drop with nothing done. Join a group with friends, and someone else notices too. Small, daily and seen beats big and someday.

The usual way

  • A big new timetable that starts "from Monday"
  • Miss one day and the whole plan feels broken
  • Nobody notices when the start keeps moving

With Padhai Timer

  • 10 minutes a day keeps your streak going
  • The D-day count shows how many days are left
  • Your group sees your study time today

Why it helps

  • A tiny start is hard to skip, even on a bad day
  • Once you start, carrying on is easier
  • A streak gives you a reason to come back tomorrow
  • Friends seeing your time keeps you honest

Don't wait for Monday. Set your exam date, tap one subject and study for 10 minutes tonight. That's day 1.
