"I studied 14 hours today." Why other people's numbers make you feel behind, and what a fair number looks like

In one line Most big study numbers you see online are never checked. Comparing your honest hours with unchecked ones only makes you want to quit. Compare with numbers that follow the same rules as yours.

It's 11 at night. You did a solid 6 hours and you feel good, until you open your phone. Someone posted a screenshot: 14 hours. Someone else says they never sleep before 2. Suddenly your 6 hours feel like nothing.

But think about what that 14 includes. A timer left running through lunch. A break that turned into an hour. Nobody checked. Your 6 hours were real; their 14 might not be. Comparing the two is like comparing your marks with someone who checked their own paper.

In Padhai Timer's study hall, everyone follows the same rule. Every 90 minutes the app asks "Still studying?". If nobody taps within 5 minutes, the timer stops at the moment it asked, and the extra time isn't counted. So the hours you see next to other nicknames passed the same check as yours.

That changes how comparing feels. Instead of a number that makes you want to give up, you see people like you, preparing for your exam, today. Beating yesterday's you by 30 minutes becomes a goal you can actually hit.

The usual way

  • Screenshots of 14 hours that nobody checked
  • A timer left running through breaks still counts
  • You feel behind even on a good day

With Padhai Timer

  • Every 90 minutes: "Still studying?"

Why it helps

  • You compare with checked hours, not boasts
  • A good day finally feels like a good day
  • Small daily goals feel reachable
  • Forgetting to stop the timer won't inflate your day

Your honest hours are worth more than anyone's screenshot. Tonight, compare with people who play by the same rules.
